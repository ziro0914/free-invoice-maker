Invoicing tips for caterers

  • State the final guest count the invoice is based on and when it was locked.
  • Itemize each menu package with the per-person price.
  • Break out staff by role, hours and rate, and list rentals separately.
  • Define the service charge and say whether it is a gratuity.
  • Show the deposit as a credit and note what it covered.

Typical line items

  • Buffet menu, per person (final count 50 guests)
  • Servers (2 staff x 5 hours)
  • Equipment rental (chafing dishes, serveware)
  • Service charge (flat, as per contract)
  • Deposit received (credit)

FAQ

What if the guest count changes?

Many caterers set a minimum and lock the final count a few days before the event. Put that policy in your quote and show the count billed on the invoice.

Is a service charge the same as a tip?

Not necessarily. Treatment of service charges and gratuities, including tax, varies by location, so state clearly what it is and check local rules.

How should I show a deposit?

As a credit line against the total, noting what it covers and whether any part is non-refundable per your contract.
