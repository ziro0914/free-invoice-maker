Invoicing tips for musicians

  • List the performance date, venue, set times and number of musicians so the client can match the invoice to the booking.
  • Charge for custom arrangements or learning special requests as their own line.
  • For session work, state whether the fee is a full buyout or whether you retain royalty or credit arrangements.
  • Take a deposit for private events and set the balance due before the performance date.

Typical line items

  • String trio: ceremony and cocktail hour (2.5 hours)
  • Custom arrangement of requested song
  • Amplification for outdoor ceremony
  • Travel (per mile, round trip)

FAQ

How should musicians invoice for a wedding?

Include the date, venue, which parts of the day you're covering and the number of musicians. List add-ons like learning a special song, amplification or extra time separately. Most wedding musicians take a deposit at booking and the balance before the event, with overtime invoiced afterward if the couple asks you to keep playing.

What should a session musician's invoice include?

List the studio date, session hours or tracks recorded, the artist or project name and the agreed rate. State whether the fee is a buyout or whether you are entitled to royalties, credits or union scale terms. Producers often handle several sessions a week, so precise details help them pay you quickly.

How do I split payment with my band on an invoice?

The simplest approach is one invoice from the bandleader or band entity to the client at a single package price, with the leader paying members privately. If players invoice individually, coordinate so the client receives clear, consistent invoices. Either way, agree on splits in writing before the gig to avoid awkward conversations afterward.
