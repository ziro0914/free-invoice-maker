Invoicing tips for truck drivers

  • Put the load number, rate confirmation number and pickup and delivery dates on every invoice, and attach the signed BOL.
  • Bill accessorials such as detention, layover, lumper fees and TONU as separate lines, with timestamps or receipts.
  • Invoice as soon as the load delivers; brokers often pay on 30-day terms, and quick-pay programs or factoring can speed this up for a fee.
  • If you factor invoices, make sure the remittance details match your factoring agreement so payments aren't misdirected.

FAQ

What documents should I send with a trucking invoice?

Typically the signed bill of lading or proof of delivery, the rate confirmation, and receipts for accessorials like lumper fees or scale tickets. Brokers often won't process payment without the POD. Scan clear copies, label them with the load number and send them with the invoice in one email or upload.

How do I bill detention time?

Check your rate confirmation for the detention terms, often a set hourly rate after two free hours at the shipper or receiver. Record arrival and departure times, ideally with check-in and check-out signatures or ELD data. Add detention as a separate line with the hours and rate, and notify the broker promptly when it starts.

Should I invoice per mile or per load?

Follow whatever the rate confirmation says. Brokered freight is usually quoted as a flat rate per load, sometimes with fuel surcharge broken out, while dedicated or contract work might pay per mile. Your invoice should mirror the agreed structure exactly, since any mismatch is likely to delay payment or trigger a short-pay.
