Invoicing tips for personal trainers

  • Show sessions purchased, used and remaining, plus the package expiry date, on each invoice.
  • State your late-cancellation policy, typically 24 hours, and bill or deduct missed sessions consistently.
  • Bill travel to clients' homes or parks as a flat fee if it falls outside your standard area.
  • Collect package payments up front; prepaid packages improve attendance and cash flow.

FAQ

Should personal trainers charge per session or per package?

Packages are popular because they secure commitment and give you predictable income, often at a slightly lower per-session price. Single sessions suit trial clients or occasional check-ins. On the invoice, show the per-session value inside a package so clients understand the discount and the cost of extra sessions if they need more.

How do I invoice online coaching clients?

Bill monthly in advance for a defined service, such as a training program, weekly check-ins and messaging support. Describe exactly what is included so clients know what to expect. Many coaches use recurring card payments, but a clear monthly invoice is still useful as a record and for clients who pay by bank transfer.

What should I do about expired sessions?

Set an expiry period, such as 90 days for a 10-pack, and state it on the invoice and in your terms. When sessions expire, you can forfeit them as agreed or offer a one-time extension as goodwill. Clear written terms make the conversation easier and protect your schedule from a backlog of old sessions.
