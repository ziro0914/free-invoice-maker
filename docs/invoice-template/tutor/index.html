Invoicing tips for tutors

  • Bill monthly with each session dated, or sell prepaid packages of 5-10 sessions to smooth out income and reduce no-shows.
  • Put your cancellation policy on the invoice, such as full charge for cancellations under 24 hours.
  • Charge travel time or a flat travel fee for in-home sessions beyond a set radius.
  • Keep group session pricing per student and list each student on the invoice when families share a session.

FAQ

Should tutors bill before or after sessions?

Both are common. Billing in advance for a month or a prepaid package reduces late payments and last-minute cancellations, while billing after sessions suits occasional or irregular students. Many tutors require prepayment for exam-prep packages and bill weekly regulars monthly in arrears. Choose one approach and state it clearly in your terms.

How do I handle missed tutoring sessions on an invoice?

If your policy charges for late cancellations or no-shows, include the session as a normal line with a note such as late cancellation. Being transparent keeps the relationship positive. Some tutors waive the first missed session as goodwill, and you can show that as a zero-priced line to signal the policy still applies.

Can parents use a tutoring invoice for reimbursement?

Sometimes. Employers, school districts or education savings programs may reimburse tutoring if the invoice shows dates, subjects, hours, the student's name and your details. Requirements differ by program and country, so ask the parent what their provider needs and check local rules before describing the service in a particular way.
