Invoicing tips for djs

  • Take a non-refundable retainer to lock the date, and invoice the balance before the event so you don't chase payment after the last song.
  • Set and state an overtime rate in advance, then bill it after the event as a separate dated line.
  • List add-ons like uplighting, ceremony audio or a photo booth separately so clients can see what their package includes.
  • Include the venue, event date and set times on the invoice to avoid confusion when you have several bookings close together.

FAQ

When should a DJ get paid for a wedding?

Most wedding DJs take a retainer at booking and collect the remaining balance one to two weeks before the event, so payment is settled before the day. Overtime requested on the night is invoiced afterward. Avoid relying on cash in an envelope at the end of the night, which is easy to forget during a busy reception.

How should DJs invoice for overtime?

Agree on an hourly overtime rate in your contract and confirm with the client or planner on the night before extending. Afterward, send a short invoice or add a line to the final invoice showing the extra time, rate and total. Some DJs require overtime to be paid on the spot before continuing.

Should I charge travel for out-of-town gigs?

Yes. Beyond a local radius, many DJs charge per mile or a flat travel fee, and for distant events add accommodation if the gig ends late. List travel separately so clients understand it is cost recovery rather than a higher performance fee. Include load-in charges for venues with difficult access.
