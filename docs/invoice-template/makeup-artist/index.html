Invoicing tips for makeup artists

  • Take a non-refundable booking fee to reserve the date and show it as received on the final invoice.
  • Charge early-start fees for call times before a set hour, such as 7am, and list them separately.
  • Bill on-location travel by distance or zone, and include parking or tolls if applicable.
  • For large bridal parties, list each service per person so costs can be split easily.

FAQ

Should I take a deposit for bridal makeup bookings?

Yes, most artists require a non-refundable deposit or retainer, often 30-50%, to secure a wedding date, since you'll turn away other work for that day. Record the deposit on the final invoice and set the balance due on or before the wedding morning. Spell out what happens if the party size changes.

How do I invoice a commercial or editorial shoot?

Commercial and editorial work is usually billed at a half-day or full-day rate rather than per face, with overtime charged hourly after the agreed time. Add kit fees, assistant fees and travel as separate lines. Production companies often require an invoice with their job number and may pay net 30, so ask early.

Can I charge for a makeup trial separately?

Yes. Many artists bill trials as a separate service on their own invoice, or include them in a bridal package. If paid separately, note the trial date on the final invoice for reference. Charging for trials values your time, product and planning, and it filters out clients who are only shopping for ideas.
