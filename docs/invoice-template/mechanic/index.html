Invoicing tips for mechanics

  • Record year, make, model, VIN and mileage on every invoice; it becomes part of the vehicle's service history.
  • List parts with part numbers and note whether they are new OEM, aftermarket, used or rebuilt.
  • Bill labor by job using book times or actual hours, and be consistent so customers can compare estimates with invoices.
  • Document recommended repairs the customer declined, which protects you if a related issue appears later.

FAQ

What should an auto repair invoice include?

Include your shop details, customer name, vehicle year, make, model, VIN and odometer reading, then each repair with parts, part numbers and labor. Add shop fees, disposal and any warranty information. Some states and provinces require specific wording or written estimates before work begins, so check local rules for your area.

Should mechanics charge book time or actual time?

Many shops bill flat-rate book hours from labor guides, so customers pay a predictable amount regardless of how quickly the technician works. Others bill actual time, which suits unusual or diagnostic work. Either way, state the labor rate and hours per job on the invoice so the customer can compare it with the estimate.

Can I charge a diagnostic fee if the customer declines the repair?

Yes, diagnostic time is real work, and most shops charge for it whether or not the repair goes ahead. Some credit the fee toward the repair if the customer proceeds. Explain the policy before starting diagnostics and show it as a separate line so the customer knows exactly what they are paying for.
