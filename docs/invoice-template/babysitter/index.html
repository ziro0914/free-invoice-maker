Invoicing tips for babysitters

  • Record start and end times for each booking; it avoids rounding disputes after late nights.
  • Charge more for extra children, after-midnight hours, holidays or last-minute bookings, and list each separately.
  • Bill mileage or a flat fee if you drive children to school or activities in your own car.
  • Agree on a minimum booking length, often 3 hours, and note it in your terms.

FAQ

Do babysitters really need invoices?

For occasional evenings, families often pay on the spot. But regular sitters, nanny shares and families using a dependent care account or employer childcare benefit often need a written record of dates, hours and amounts. An invoice also helps you track your own income and spot unpaid hours if a family pays irregularly.

How should I charge for multiple children?

A common approach is a base hourly rate for the first child plus a few dollars per hour for each additional child. Listing the extra-child charge as its own line keeps the calculation transparent. For nanny shares, invoice each family separately for their share of the hours so nobody has to work out the split.

Can I charge a late cancellation fee?

Yes, if you agree on it in advance. Many sitters charge a portion of the booked hours, or a minimum amount, when families cancel within 24 hours, since you've turned down other work. Include the policy on your invoice and mention it when accepting regular bookings so it never comes as a surprise.
