Photography invoices do more than request payment: they document what the client actually bought. Invoicing tips for photographers

  • State the image usage rights on the invoice itself (medium, territory, duration) so the license is documented alongside the payment record.
  • Collect a non-refundable retainer, typically 25-50%, to hold the date, and show it as already paid when you issue the final invoice.
  • Bill rush delivery, extra retouching rounds and additional images as separate lines rather than folding them into the session fee.
  • Release full-resolution or unwatermarked files only after the balance clears, and say so in your payment terms.

Typical line items

  • Portrait session (2 hours, on location)
  • Professional editing and retouching (per image)
  • Commercial usage license (1 year, web and social)
  • Travel (per mile)

FAQ

Should I charge sales tax on photography invoices?

It depends on where you work. Some US states treat prints, digital image deliveries or both as taxable, while others tax only physical products; in the UK, Canada and Australia, registration usually depends on your turnover. Check local rules or ask an accountant, then add the tax rate in the generator if it applies to you.

How do I invoice for a wedding paid in installments?

Issue one invoice per payment milestone, each referencing the same contract and showing the total package price, amounts already received and the remaining balance. A common schedule is a retainer at booking, a second payment a few months out, and the final balance two to four weeks before the wedding so nothing is outstanding on the day.

Do I need to list every edited image?

No. One line showing the number of edited images and the per-image rate is clearer than dozens of rows. Itemize only when pricing differs, such as standard edits versus advanced retouching, or when a client ordered specific frames from a proofing gallery and needs the invoice to match their selection.
