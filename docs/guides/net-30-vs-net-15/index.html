Net 30 vs Net 15: Which Payment Terms Should Freelancers Use?

"Net 30" and "Net 15" are two of the most common payment terms on freelance invoices, and picking the wrong one can leave you funding a client's project for a month. This guide explains what each term means, how they affect your cash flow, and how to decide which to offer. If you are new to the topic, start with our overview of invoice payment terms.

What Net 15 and Net 30 mean

"Net 15" means the full amount is due 15 days after the invoice date. "Net 30" means 30 days. The count normally starts on the invoice date (or the date you send it), so send the invoice promptly. It is a good habit to also write the actual due date, such as "Due 14 November", so there is no argument about how the days are counted.

Why the gap matters: a simple cash-flow example

Imagine you invoice one client $4,000 on the 1st of each month, and the client always pays on the last day allowed.

TermPayment arrivesDays you wait
Net 15Around the 16th15
Net 30Around the 31st30

Same $4,000, but with Net 30 you wait 15 extra days for it, and that comes on top of the time you already spent doing the work. If your rent, software and tax payments do not wait, you are effectively lending the client that money for two more weeks. Real clients often pay later than the due date, so the true gap can be longer than the terms suggest.

When Net 15 makes sense

  • Small clients and individuals. They usually have no formal approval process, so there is little reason to wait 30 days.
  • New clients. Shorter terms limit your exposure while you learn how reliably they pay.
  • One-off or short projects. There is less relationship to protect and less reason to extend credit.
  • When your own cash is tight. Faster payment helps you cover costs and plan what to set aside for taxes.

When Net 30 makes sense

  • Larger companies. Many have accounts-payable processes that pay in batches and may require 30-day terms by policy.
  • Ongoing retainers. A steady monthly client may be worth the longer wait if it brings predictable work.
  • Competitive situations. Sometimes accepting Net 30 is the price of winning the job, as long as you have the cash buffer to cope with it.

It is common advice to offer Net 15 as your default and accept Net 30 when a client's policy requires it. Some freelancers also use "due on receipt" for very small invoices, or ask for a deposit before starting. Our proforma invoice guide covers one way to get paid before work begins.

How to negotiate terms

  1. Put your preferred term in the proposal or contract so it is agreed before work starts, not discovered on the first invoice.
  2. If a client asks for Net 30, consider a trade. For example, a deposit up front, milestone invoices on longer projects, or a small discount for paying early.
  3. Split big projects. Invoicing in stages means you never carry a large unpaid balance, whatever the term.
  4. Ask about their process. Sometimes a client only needs a PO number or a specific contact to pay faster.

Add a late-payment clause

Shorter terms only help if clients actually pay by the due date. Say on the invoice what happens when they do not, and agree it in advance. What about Net 7, Net 45 and Net 60?

Net 7 is sometimes used for small, quick jobs, though some clients find it tight. Net 45 and Net 60 appear mostly with large organisations and can strain a freelancer's cash flow badly. If a client proposes them, you can ask for a shorter term, a deposit, or milestone invoices instead. Whether you can refuse is a business decision: weigh the value of the work against the cost of waiting. Some countries also have rules on maximum or default payment periods, particularly between businesses, so check your local rules or official government guidance.

Common mistakes

  • Not writing a due date. "Net 30" alone can be read different ways, especially if the invoice is sent late.
  • Sending invoices late. Every day you delay sending pushes the payment date back.
  • Accepting vague terms by email. Get the agreed term in writing.
  • Not following up. Set a reminder for the day after the due date and use a polite template.

Quick checklist

  • Choose a default term (many freelancers start with Net 15).
  • Write the exact due date, not only "Net 15".
  • Agree the term before work begins.
  • Accept longer terms only when you can afford the wait.
  • Include a late-payment clause you have checked against local rules.
  • Send invoices promptly, because the clock starts at the invoice date.

This guide is general information, not legal or financial advice. Customs and rules differ by country, industry and client.
