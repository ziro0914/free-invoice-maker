How do I calculate the cost of a 2/10 net 30 early payment discount?

Divide the discount by what you still receive (2 / 98), then multiply by 365 divided by the days you are speeding up payment (30 - 10 = 20). That is 2/98 x 365/20, about 37.24% a year. Some textbooks use a 360-day year, which gives a slightly lower figure, and compounding raises it.

Is offering an early payment discount worth it?

It depends on what your own money costs. If you would otherwise borrow at a rate below the annualized cost, the discount costs more than it saves; if cash is tight and financing is expensive, it can help. For example, on 1,000 at a 12% cost of money, 2/10 net 30 gives away 20 to save about 6.44 of interest. Smaller discounts such as 1% are often tried first. Check your contract and local rules, and treat this as an estimate, not financial advice.
