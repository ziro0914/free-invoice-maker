How do I calculate the cost of scope creep?

Divide the fixed price by the hours you really worked to get your effective rate, and multiply the overrun hours by your target rate for the unpaid value. For example, a 3,000 project estimated at 30 hours but taking 42 pays 71.43 per hour instead of 100, and the 12 extra hours are worth 1,200 at a 100 rate.

How can I stop scope creep on fixed-price work?

Common practice is to list deliverables and a limited number of revision rounds in writing, and to quote changes outside that list as separate paid change requests. Some freelancers also add a contingency to the estimate; sources suggest figures from roughly 10% to 30%, but the right amount depends on the project and client.
